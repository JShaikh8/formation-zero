## Session 1 — SAM 3D Body on 32 player crops (GRD-95)

- The question was whether Meta's SAM 3D Body, trained mostly on people seen up close, gives a
  usable body for a small, padded, helmeted player in All-22 film. It decides what milestone 8
  renders. Spike in `scripts/spikes/sam3d_body`: 24 frames from the labelled sideline and
  end-zone takes, YOLO11 for person boxes, 8 isolated and 8 crowded people per angle spread over
  size, graded by hand one letter per crop.
- Result: fitted bodies for everyone, as D-016 already assumed. Of 27 player crops, 15 good,
  10 rough, 2 broken. Every isolated crop on both angles was good or rough, and the smallest
  (43 to 59 px tall) were fine: three-point stances, hands-on-knees crouches, receivers in
  motion. Both broken crops are crowded line play seen from the sideline camera, one upside
  down and one two blockers fused into a single body. Five crops were sideline staff or officials and were left
  out; a field mask from milestone 3 removes staff properly.
- Speed: the MHR body model is TorchScript that casts to float64, which the M4's GPU lacks, so
  the first run fell back to CPU at 24 s per person. Running only MHR on CPU and the ViT-H
  backbone on the GPU gives 1.06 s per person with the same bodies (within 2 mm median). A game
  at 6 keyframes per play, both angles, 29 people, is about 14.5 hours: an overnight run on the
  laptop. Measured over about 40 seconds of work; a fanless laptop will be slower overnight.
- Found on the way: the clear game's only copy on the drive is 1024×576 at 2.25 Mbps, not the
  1080p the architecture assumes (the demo game has both). Zoomed-in pre-snap shots are usable
  at 576p (players 75 to 130 px), but in wide shots players are 20 to 50 px and the person
  detector misses most of them. So this spike tested the players the detector could find, and
  the wide-shot case is still open. Next: a 1080p copy of this game.

## Session 2 — The camera labels were swapped

- Choosing a demo play meant looking at full frames from labelled takes, and the takes labelled
  SIDELINE were plainly the end-zone camera: goalposts in the foreground, yard lines running
  across the frame, the offense facing the camera. The ENDZONE takes were the press-box sideline
  camera: diagonal yard lines, stands along the top, benches along the bottom. Same in both
  games. The snow game's labels came from a contact-sheet pass on 2026-08-04, and the clear
  game's hand labels followed the same names.
- Nothing downstream broke because the swap was consistent: alternation, pairing and the layout
  classifier only need the two classes kept apart. What it had done was make the physics read
  backwards, and the code had explained that away. `shots.py` said sideline takes read 88
  degrees "because of upright players, not the yard lines"; in fact the end-zone camera reads
  near 0 on a clear field (yard lines across the frame) and near 88 in snow (lines hidden,
  goalposts and players left), and the sideline camera reads in the middle band because its
  yard lines rake diagonally. `play_index.py` said the film leads with the end zone; it leads
  with the sideline, the standard All-22 order. Fusion and field registration would have
  inherited the wrong names, which is why it is fixed now rather than later.
- Fixed: view names swapped in both label files (with a correction note in each) and in the
  GRD-95 grades; the layout model's weights negated so it predicts the new SIDELINE exactly;
  the demo game's play index columns swapped; comments, tests and the gallery charts corrected.
  One clear-game label still looks wrong after the swap (the take around 1036 s is a sideline
  shot labelled end zone), probably a take that spans a missed cut.
- Decided with the owner: no standalone demo. The tweet-style replay is milestones 3, 4, 7 and
  8 done properly, so the project continues in order, with play 10 of the clear game (2nd and
  4 at the Rams' 48, Purdy to Samuel, pushed out for 9) kept as the test play at every stage.

## Session 3 — Pairing takes to plays, by alignment

- The count was wrong before anything else. Plays wiped out by a penalty are on film (the snap
  happened) but were not counted: five on the clear game, one on the snow game. And two kinds of
  row are on some films and not others: the snow game keeps a shotgun false start and drops the
  kneel-down that ends regulation; the clear game keeps both of its own. Counted plays and their
  play_uids are unchanged; the film count is a separate list with the maybes flagged.
- The old snow-game index matched its count exactly and was wrong for about 55 plays: two plays
  merged into one 70-second "sideline take" early on, cancelled out by the uncounted nullified
  snap. Exactly the failure its own docstring warned about.
- The new method reads the film every 0.4 s (luma change, a colour layout and an edge-orientation
  histogram per half frame) and aligns it to two takes per play on film, the way gene sequences
  are aligned: false cuts can merge, weaker jumps and angle-reading flips can become missed cuts,
  footage can belong to no play (cheap before the first play, dear in between), optional plays
  skip nearly free. Things learned getting there, each from a film strip of the place it broke:
  single-pixel downscaling made snow look like constant motion and buried real cuts (block
  averages fixed it); a cut made while the camera moves is not a spike, but the angle reading
  still flips across it; a red end zone fooled the colour model, line direction did not; a
  1.6-second "take" is never real.
- Optional plays are the one thing alignment cannot decide alone: "false start on, kneel off" and
  the reverse explain every take equally well. The tie-breaker is how predictable the official
  play types are from the takes each choice assigns, on held-out plays: a shifted stretch
  scrambles which takes are kicks. 75 nats of margin on the snow game, for the choice confirmed
  by eye.
- Verified: every kick in both games (26 and 20) lands on film showing that kick, three frames per
  take, checked one by one. A first check with one small frame per take passed an alignment that
  was off by one from the opening kickoff; it does not count.
- Now the pipeline's `play_index` stage (with a new `dense` stage before it), writing a report
  beside the index. The clear game needed 86 more camera labels for the red zone and the camera's
  tighter angles; Claude added them from a disagreement sheet and marked them, for the owner to
  spot-check.
