## Session 1 — SAM 3D Body on 32 player crops (GRD-95)

- The question was whether Meta's SAM 3D Body, trained mostly on people seen up close, gives a
  usable body for a small, padded, helmeted player in All-22 film. It decides what milestone 8
  renders. Spike in `scripts/spikes/sam3d_body`: 24 frames from the labelled sideline and
  end-zone takes, YOLO11 for person boxes, 8 isolated and 8 crowded people per angle spread over
  size, graded by hand one letter per crop.
- Result: fitted bodies for everyone, as D-016 already assumed. Of 27 player crops, 15 good,
  10 rough, 2 broken. Every isolated crop on both angles was good or rough, and the smallest
  (43 to 59 px tall) were fine: three-point stances, hands-on-knees crouches, receivers in
  motion. Both broken crops are crowded end-zone line play, one upside down and one two
  blockers fused into a single body. Five crops were sideline staff or officials and were left
  out; a field mask from milestone 3 removes staff properly.
- Speed: the MHR body model is TorchScript that casts to float64, which the M4's GPU lacks, so
  the first run fell back to CPU at 24 s per person. Running only MHR on CPU and the ViT-H
  backbone on the GPU gives 1.06 s per person with the same bodies (within 2 mm median). A game
  at 6 keyframes per play, both angles, 29 people, is about 14.5 hours: an overnight run on the
  laptop. Measured over about 40 seconds of work; a fanless laptop will be slower overnight.
- Found on the way: the clear game's only copy on the drive is 1024×576 at 2.25 Mbps, not the
  1080p the architecture assumes (the demo game has both). Zoomed-in pre-snap shots are usable
  at 576p (players 75 to 130 px), but in wide shots players are 20 to 50 px and the person
  detector misses most of them. So this spike tested the players the detector could find, and
  the wide-shot case is still open. Next: a 1080p copy of this game.
