## Session 1 — SAM 3D Body on 32 player crops (GRD-95)

- The question was whether Meta's SAM 3D Body, trained mostly on people seen up close, gives a
  usable body for a small, padded, helmeted player in All-22 film. It decides what milestone 8
  renders. Spike in `scripts/spikes/sam3d_body`: 24 frames from the labelled sideline and
  end-zone takes, YOLO11 for person boxes, 8 isolated and 8 crowded people per angle spread over
  size, graded by hand one letter per crop.
- Result: fitted bodies for everyone, as D-016 already assumed. Of 27 player crops, 15 good,
  10 rough, 2 broken. Every isolated crop on both angles was good or rough, and the smallest
  (43 to 59 px tall) were fine: three-point stances, hands-on-knees crouches, receivers in
  motion. Both broken crops are crowded line play seen from the sideline camera, one upside
  down and one two blockers fused into a single body. Five crops were sideline staff or officials and were left
  out; a field mask from milestone 3 removes staff properly.
- Speed: the MHR body model is TorchScript that casts to float64, which the M4's GPU lacks, so
  the first run fell back to CPU at 24 s per person. Running only MHR on CPU and the ViT-H
  backbone on the GPU gives 1.06 s per person with the same bodies (within 2 mm median). A game
  at 6 keyframes per play, both angles, 29 people, is about 14.5 hours: an overnight run on the
  laptop. Measured over about 40 seconds of work; a fanless laptop will be slower overnight.
- Found on the way: the clear game's only copy on the drive is 1024×576 at 2.25 Mbps, not the
  1080p the architecture assumes (the demo game has both). Zoomed-in pre-snap shots are usable
  at 576p (players 75 to 130 px), but in wide shots players are 20 to 50 px and the person
  detector misses most of them. So this spike tested the players the detector could find, and
  the wide-shot case is still open. Next: a 1080p copy of this game.

## Session 2 — The camera labels were swapped

- Choosing a demo play meant looking at full frames from labelled takes, and the takes labelled
  SIDELINE were plainly the end-zone camera: goalposts in the foreground, yard lines running
  across the frame, the offense facing the camera. The ENDZONE takes were the press-box sideline
  camera: diagonal yard lines, stands along the top, benches along the bottom. Same in both
  games. The snow game's labels came from a contact-sheet pass on 2026-08-04, and the clear
  game's hand labels followed the same names.
- Nothing downstream broke because the swap was consistent: alternation, pairing and the layout
  classifier only need the two classes kept apart. What it had done was make the physics read
  backwards, and the code had explained that away. `shots.py` said sideline takes read 88
  degrees "because of upright players, not the yard lines"; in fact the end-zone camera reads
  near 0 on a clear field (yard lines across the frame) and near 88 in snow (lines hidden,
  goalposts and players left), and the sideline camera reads in the middle band because its
  yard lines rake diagonally. `play_index.py` said the film leads with the end zone; it leads
  with the sideline, the standard All-22 order. Fusion and field registration would have
  inherited the wrong names, which is why it is fixed now rather than later.
- Fixed: view names swapped in both label files (with a correction note in each) and in the
  GRD-95 grades; the layout model's weights negated so it predicts the new SIDELINE exactly;
  the demo game's play index columns swapped; comments, tests and the gallery charts corrected.
  One clear-game label still looks wrong after the swap (the take around 1036 s is a sideline
  shot labelled end zone), probably a take that spans a missed cut.
- Decided with the owner: no standalone demo. The tweet-style replay is milestones 3, 4, 7 and
  8 done properly, so the project continues in order, with play 10 of the clear game (2nd and
  4 at the Rams' 48, Purdy to Samuel, pushed out for 9) kept as the test play at every stage.

## Session 3 — Pairing takes to plays, by alignment

- The count was wrong before anything else. Plays wiped out by a penalty are on film (the snap
  happened) but were not counted: five on the clear game, one on the snow game. And two kinds of
  row are on some films and not others: the snow game keeps a shotgun false start and drops the
  kneel-down that ends regulation; the clear game keeps both of its own. Counted plays and their
  play_uids are unchanged; the film count is a separate list with the maybes flagged.
- The old snow-game index matched its count exactly and was wrong for about 55 plays: two plays
  merged into one 70-second "sideline take" early on, cancelled out by the uncounted nullified
  snap. Exactly the failure its own docstring warned about.
- The new method reads the film every 0.4 s (luma change, a colour layout and an edge-orientation
  histogram per half frame) and aligns it to two takes per play on film, the way gene sequences
  are aligned: false cuts can merge, weaker jumps and angle-reading flips can become missed cuts,
  footage can belong to no play (cheap before the first play, dear in between), optional plays
  skip nearly free. Things learned getting there, each from a film strip of the place it broke:
  single-pixel downscaling made snow look like constant motion and buried real cuts (block
  averages fixed it); a cut made while the camera moves is not a spike, but the angle reading
  still flips across it; a red end zone fooled the colour model, line direction did not; a
  1.6-second "take" is never real.
- Optional plays are the one thing alignment cannot decide alone: "false start on, kneel off" and
  the reverse explain every take equally well. The tie-breaker is how predictable the official
  play types are from the takes each choice assigns, on held-out plays: a shifted stretch
  scrambles which takes are kicks. 75 nats of margin on the snow game, for the choice confirmed
  by eye.
- Verified: every kick in both games (26 and 20) lands on film showing that kick, three frames per
  take, checked one by one. A first check with one small frame per take passed an alignment that
  was off by one from the opening kickoff; it does not count.
- Now the pipeline's `play_index` stage (with a new `dense` stage before it), writing a report
  beside the index. The clear game needed 86 more camera labels for the red zone and the camera's
  tighter angles; Claude added them from a disagreement sheet and marked them, for the owner to
  spot-check.
- Tried and failed, written down so it is not retried as is: indexing a game with none of its own
  camera labels. Starting from the other game's line-direction model (55% per sample) and
  self-training on the alignment's own output settles at 67-75% per sample, and the alignments
  match the verified index on 7% (clear game) and 32-64%, unstable (snow game) of plays. Every new
  game needs its ~40 sheet labels for now. The likelier fix is a pretrained image embedding that
  sees camera geometry rather than colour; it needs another full pass over the film.
- Also built while waiting on SAM 3: the frame sampler (GRD-28, 2,000 frames per game across
  angle, phase and half; every play used) and the teacher review page (GRD-30), both ready for
  the teacher's first boxes.

## Session 4 — SAM 3 measured, the labelling loop built

- SAM 3 measured on this laptop (GRD-32, a background agent, `scripts/spikes/sam3_throughput`):
  it runs on the GPU once four CUDA-only details are shimmed, at 0.29 frames a second rested and
  0.16 once the fanless Air heats up, 6.3 s a frame. A whole game would take 236 hours, twelve
  times the plan's guess, so SAM 3 stays the teacher and never the runtime detector (D-014, by a
  wider margin). 576p costs the same as 1080p: the model squashes every frame to 1008x1008.
  Players and referees look right on six frames; the football prompt does not work.
- The labelling loop of section 7.1 now exists end to end, each piece with tests: the sampler
  (2,000 frames per game, extracted to the film drive: 759 MB and 240 MB), the SAM 3 teacher
  (batched prompts, bfloat16, resumable, duplicate and giant-ball filters), the review page
  (one key per frame, least confident first), and student training with the disagreement sheet
  for the next round. The teacher is labelling both games overnight, about 7 hours.
- Not done yet: a field mask, so sideline staff come back as players; the ball, which needs its
  own approach (section 7.1's pass/run trajectory filter, or tiles).

## Session 5 — the teacher's first game, checked; the review page made to work

- The SAM 3 teacher finished the snow game: 2,000 frames in 3.4 hours (6.1 s a frame), 19
  players and 1.4 referees a frame on average. 49ers–Rams is running.
- Checked by eye on 12 random frames (both angles, both halves) and on the most crowded ones:
  players and referees are nearly all boxed, including small far-side players, with few false
  boxes. The errors are bench players and staff in the widest kickoff shots (one frame had 37
  "players"), the odd box drawn around two players, and people cut by the frame edge.
- The ball is better than the throughput spike said. 395 balls in 313 frames; on 48 checked by
  eye, 21 of 24 at 0.4 or more were the ball and none clearly were not, against 10 of 24 below
  0.4. So it is precise but finds the ball in about one frame in eight, mostly at the snap and in
  the quarterback's hands. The detections file now cuts balls at 0.4; the run's log keeps
  everything from 0.3, so the cut can move without running SAM 3 again.
- The review page had never been opened in a browser. Driving it in headless Chrome showed it
  drew no boxes and would have decoded all 2,000 full-size frames at once. Rebuilt: boxes are SVG
  over images that load as they scroll, and space enlarges a frame, where a click removes one box,
  so a kickoff frame with a boxed bench player can still be kept (D-015 amended). Keys, enlarge,
  remove, undo, reload and export all checked in Chrome, and the export imports.
- Open question for the student (GRD-31): with the ball found in one frame in eight, most
  approved frames show a ball that is not labelled, which teaches the student that balls are
  background. Likely answer: train the student on players and referees, and give the ball its own
  model from the frames where it is labelled.

## Session 6 — round 0, and the first pieces of milestones 3 and 4

- The teacher finished 49ers–Rams: 21.6 players, 2.8 referees and 0.23 balls a frame (snow game
  19.0, 1.4, 0.13).
- Round 0 of the student (GRD-31) trained on the teacher's unreviewed boxes, in two folds by play:
  each student learns half the plays and scores the half it never saw, so the review page can put
  the frames the two disagree on first without the student grading its own homework. Against the
  teacher on held-out plays: mAP50 0.94, players found 90-91% at 95-97% precision, referees about
  80%. Disagreement averages 3.2 boxes a frame on the snow game and 5.5 on 49ers–Rams (576p, small
  wide-shot players). The top of each page is what a person should look at: wide kickoff shots with
  benches, snow pile-ups, single boxes drawn around several players.
- Training first ran at 1280 px, batch 8, validating every epoch, and the 16 GB laptop swapped
  itself to a standstill. 960 px at batch 4, validating once at the end, runs 8 epochs in 1.3 to
  1.6 hours a fold. About 18 GB of swap was held by other open apps throughout.
- Milestone 3 groundwork (GRD-33 to 36): detector, tracker and team-classifier protocols; a YOLO
  detector; ByteTrack per label (supervision removed ByteTrack in 0.30, so pinned below it) with a
  per-track summary of coverage, gaps and fragments; tracking straight from the source film; an
  overlay video per take. The COCO stand-in finds 80% of the teacher's players on 60 snow frames
  and adds 5 to 9 other people a frame, which is why the student replaces it.
- Team split by uniform colour (GRD-35, a background agent, rechecked): shirt and pants colour with
  the crop's own field colour down-weighted, which is what makes it work on snow. About 90-95% of
  single crops right on the hardest sheets; tracks vote, so one crop's error does not decide a
  track. SigLIP not started.
- Field lines and mask (GRD-37, a background agent, reviewed): yard lines and the boundary where the
  paint stops, good on the clear game, weak in snow. Used now to suggest removals on the review page
  (boxes with feet off the field start removed, a click restores them). The first full check found it
  harmful on end-zone frames, where the end-zone paint reads as a boundary, and on the snow game,
  where 4 of 8 random frames lost players on the field. Kept for 49ers–Rams sideline frames only,
  capped at half a frame's boxes.
- Found on the way (GRD-97): some take boundaries inside the play index are seconds off, though every
  play is still on its own film. Moving them to where the 0.4 s camera reading flips was wrong 2 times
  in 3, because that reading errs on unusual shots, which is where boundaries are. Not moved; it needs
  a camera reading from yard-line direction with a hash-row test first.
