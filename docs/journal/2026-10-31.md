## Session 1 — plays 11-60, held out

- The bird's-eye chain (tracking, registration, the camera's motion, the snap, the anchor) was built
  and tuned on plays 1-10. The fair test is plays it has never seen, run as is with nothing changed
  afterwards. Plays 11-60 of 49ers-Rams were tracked from the 1080p copy (76 min for both angles)
  and their sideline takes put in yards with videos (35 min, 18,627 frames).
- Anchoring: 45 of 47 scrimmage plays pinned to the official line of scrimmage, the players' line
  0.16 yd from it (median, worst 0.9). The two kickoffs have no line and stay relative, as before.
  The two that failed failed safely. One is a pass from the 2, where the end zone fills the picture
  and the frame barely registers, so no snap is seen. The other is a play where four 49ers in the
  clump at the line were coloured as Rams by the tracker. The line came out 1.7 yd off and the check
  refused it rather than shift the take 5 yd wrong.
- By eye, one random frame from each of the 46 anchored takes: the drawn yard lines sit on the painted
  lines and numbers on all of them, from 12 s before the snap to 9 s after, including two end-zone
  corners after the whistle with the goal line on the pylon. A few far-side numbers looked off on the
  half-size sheet; full-size crops showed them on.
- At the snap the two fronts stand either side of the official line on 39 of 46 takes. The misses
  looked at are tracker team labels again (two Rams coloured as 49ers on play 42), not geometry.
  Players on the field just before the snap: 22 on 42% of frames, 20-24 on 93%, a little below plays
  1-10 measured the same way (53% and 97%).
- The shotgun rule, set after looking at plays 1-10, holds where it speaks: it decides 23 of 42 pass
  and run plays and agrees with the play-by-play's '(Shotgun)' on 21. It stays silent on 19, 17 of
  them under centre, mostly with nobody standing straight behind the ball. On plays 1-10 that empty
  case was shotgun both times, so it is not a rule for under centre by itself.
- What this says: the geometry generalises within the game. The errors left are in what the tracker
  feeds it, chiefly team colours on clumped linemen, and in frames where the end zone fills the
  picture. Both are next. The snow game is still untried.

## Session 2 — the same player on two teams, and fewer clicks

- The failures on plays 11-60 pointed at team colours, so I expected colour crops spoiled by overlap.
  The margins said otherwise. On play 29 four tracks read clearly as 49ers before the snap and
  clearly as Rams after it: the tracker had swapped ids between linemen locked together, and each
  track took one team for its whole life. Tracks are now cut where their colour changes for good, and
  the part after the cut gets a new id, so one team per track stays true for everything downstream.
- It cuts 427 of 2,904 tracks on the field, far more than I would have guessed, so I checked before
  trusting it. Of 12 random cuts, 11 were real. The twelfth, a running Ram scored weakly, led to a
  minimum strength, and 18 fresh cuts were all real.
- Rerun on plays 1-60: play 29 anchors, no other take moved, and fronts are right on 52 of 56 takes
  against 47 of 55. Players on the wrong side before the snap fell from 25 to 5. That is not a held-out
  result, since the rule was set looking at these plays.
- The review got easier. Its most disputed frames are wide shots full of bench boxes, up to two dozen
  clicks each. Judging feet in yards, as tracking now does, leaves a median of 22 player boxes on the
  first 600 frames of the 49ers-Rams page, and frames with too many fell from 203 to 72. A review
  round is about 300 frames a game, not 2,000: the next student picks the next round's frames.
- Checking my own 86 camera labels on contact sheets found three wrong, all sideline takes called end
  zone. The play index already had them right. Runpod is connected for the labelling rounds to come.

## Session 3 — the first model trained on reviewed frames

- The owner reviewed 444 frames across the two games and kept 371. Round 1 trained on them in the same
  two folds as round 0, so each model is scored only on plays it never saw. Continuing from round 0 beat
  starting fresh on both folds and both classes, at half the epochs.
- The headline was player mAP50 0.837 to 0.958, and I did not believe it. The review page puts the
  frames round 0 disputed first, so those frames flatter any new model. Player recall had in fact gone
  down a little. Round 1 learned what not to box; it did not find more people.
- So I counted on frames nobody chose, where the answer needs no label: 22 players. On random
  49ers-Rams sideline frames, boxes off the field fell from 4.2 players and 4.1 officials a frame to 0.1
  and 0.4. Players on the field stayed near 23. On the snow game nothing moved, because benches were
  never its problem.
- Officials fell from a median of 5 a frame to 1, and I took that for damage: the pre-removal must have
  stripped real wing officials from the labels. A sheet of 48 removed "referee" boxes said mostly not.
  About 6 were officials. The rest were chain crew in striped vests, coaches and camera operators the
  teacher had called referees. Restoring them all would have made the labels worse. Officials with
  feet on the field held at 1.4 a frame against 1.6.
- The frames with too many players looked fine by eye, apart from stacks of boxes on linemen at the
  line. That is the merge step rather than the labels: NMS IoU 0.7 to 0.5 takes overlapping pairs from
  2.93 a frame to 0.61, about what reviewed labels have (0.65), for a point of recall.
- Half the training plays against all of them, same steps: player mAP50 0.950 to 0.956, officials 0.850
  to 0.871. For players in these two games more frames buy little. The top of the next sheet is mostly
  piles and tight zooms, where a person could not draw clean boxes either. A third game is unmeasured.
- The next sheet would have repeated the mistake of the last one: scored against the raw teacher, every
  bench box would count as a disagreement. It now ignores boxes judged off the field, and skips frames
  the owner dropped. Yards pre-removal does not work on snow: 52 of 85 suggestions were boxes the owner
  had kept.

## Session 4 — a third game, and a good count that was partly luck

- The owner added Chargers at Chiefs, week 6, in 1080p: hard sun and shadow across the field, which
  neither of the first two games has. It is the first game no model has trained on.
- The film-window step lost the first 26 minutes of it. Shade drops the film's brightness below the
  line that keeps out dim menus. The window is now a core that grows through neighbouring windows
  that move like film and clear a lower line. The other two recordings come out unchanged. A pipeline
  test had been passing by accident: the old rule found no window on its synthetic film and processed
  the whole file, so the offset it set out to check was never tested. Now it is.
- Matching film to the play list found 143 of 144 plays, each with both takes, but kicks looked at by
  eye caught two with boundaries off by a take. Both came from cuts the detector missed. One of them was
  my own label: I named a take from its middle frame when it held both cameras. Labelling the parts by
  time fixed both.
- On 300 random frames of the new game the student boxes nobody extra on the sideline camera, and
  frames with 20-24 players reach 94% before the snap and 83% in the play, up from 88% and 69%.
- Then the test that matters: plays 1-60 of 49ers-Rams, each tracked by the model that never saw it.
  The geometry improved: fronts right on 54 of 56 plays, tracks on the benches almost gone. But exactly
  22 players before the snap fell from 43% of frames to 33%, and I had called the count a success.
- I blamed the tracker's start line and said so too early. Lowering it barely moved the count and
  cost half again as many fragments. The picture settled it: the boxes lost are real linemen buried in
  the clump, the price of the stricter merge and of lower scores. Some of round 0's good count was
  duplicates landing near 22. A count of 22 is not proof of the right 22.
- Tracking stays on round 0 for now. Forty random frames of the new game, reviewed by hand, will say
  how many players each model really misses.

## Session 5 — the first fair score

- The owner reviewed 40 random frames of Chargers-Chiefs. Nobody chose them and nothing has trained on
  that game, so it is the first score that flatters nobody: 38 frames, 711 players.
- Round 0 at the old merge: player precision 0.798, recall 0.959. The round-1 student at the new
  merge: 0.952 and 0.951. False boxes fell from 173 to 34, missed players rose from 29 to 35, and
  bootstrapped by frame that rise is within noise. Sideline frames before the snap, where the count
  had worried me, went from precision 0.74 to 0.96 with 4 players missed either way.
- The 49ers-Rams drop came from fold students trained on half the frames, and this is the final one on
  all 371. I have not tested the final student on those plays without its training frames among them,
  so the two results do not cancel; this one is simply the fairer test of what will be used.
- I looked at every wrong box, because the page can only remove boxes, so a "false" box might be a
  player SAM 3 missed. I found no clear case. Eight of the 34 are the NFL shield painted on the field,
  called a player with up to 0.86 confidence. The misses are mostly players buried in a clump or on
  the ground.
- The test is now a command, `fz-train test`, and reproduces these numbers exactly. Test frames are
  kept out of training and review sheets by code, not by remembering.
- The bird's-eye step failed its first run on the new game: no frame in yards. The first game's camera
  was hard-coded as the prior, and Arrowhead's camera is 51 yd up against 27. Each game's camera is now
  measured from its own frames. First two plays pinned to the official line within 0.12 yd.
