## Session 1 — the owner watched the videos

- The owner watched the Chargers-Chiefs videos and saw players disappearing and reappearing during
  the play. None of my counts would have shown it: they all look at the frames before the snap, or at
  single frames.
- It was common. On 50 plays, 301 s of live play, a box dropped out and came back 1,313 times, and 594
  more times a track ended with a new one starting beside it. Only 146 track ends were players leaving
  the picture.
- I assumed a blind detector. At 300 of those moments it was not: 40% had a box already held by a
  neighbour, 36% had a box the tracker threw away, and only 24% had nothing at all.
- So I tried what looked like the answer, letting weak boxes follow a track without starting one.
  Replayed on 20 plays it did nothing: flickers 444 to 482. The library multiplies overlap by score,
  so a weak box needs a tight fit either way, and there is no setting for it.
- The better idea is that this is not live. The whole play is on disk, so a second pass can join a track
  to the one that continues it, using team colour to keep locked opponents apart, and fill the gap.
  Filled boxes are marked and drawn thin, since they are an estimate.
- A join can be wrong without saying so, so I measured that first. Cutting 2,429 real tracks at
  crowded moments and asking for them back gave 99% right at 5 frames and 91% at 15, with 0.4% and
  2% wrong. Those are easier than real losses, and by eye on real ones I found 1 clearly wrong in each of
  two sheets, with about 6 in 36 too tangled to call.
- The first wrong one was a track on the painted NFL shield joined to a real Chief. Looking into that
  showed the shield is tracked on dozens of plays. I suspected the labels, but every wide box in them is a real
  player, some lying flat at 3.0 wide, so the box shape cannot delete it. The model has simply not
  seen this shield as background. A very wide track now joins nothing, and the real fix is training.
- Result: dropouts 1,313 to 36, players missing 4.5% of the time to 0.8%, frames in play with 20-24
  players 54% to 72%. About 7 visible breaks a play remain against about 38, so a player's number can still
  change.
- The pre-snap count seemed to slip, 92% to 89%. It had not: two more plays anchored, a field goal and
  an extra point showing 16 players, and on the same plays the figure is still 92%.
- Plays 121-143 had never been run, so they were the fresh test of everything from the last two days: 19
  of 21 anchored, median 0.19 yd, fronts right on 17 of 19, 21-23 players on 95% of frames before the
  snap. All 143 plays of the game now have videos. Across the game 123 of 131 scrimmage plays anchored;
  7 of the 8 misses saw no snap.

## Session 2 — every player, every frame, one play

- First run of SAM 3D Body down a whole play rather than on still crops: play 40 of Chargers-Chiefs, from
  2.4 s before the snap to the end. It is the best-tracked take of the game, 20 of 22 players on one
  track throughout against a median of 9, and it is only a short incompletion. A Mahomes run looked
  better but ends in a pile with 15 steady tracks. This is a best case, so expect worse elsewhere.
- 4,924 bodies on 226 frames took 1 h 58 min on the Air, 1.45 s a body. The spike's 1.06 s does not hold
  on full 1080p frames, and batch size barely moves it. At this rate six poses a play is about 7 hours a
  game, and every frame of a game is about 18 days.
- The real lens goes in now, read from the painted lines: 5,073 to 8,638 px against upstream's default
  of 2,203. By eye on eight full frames and crops the bodies sit on the players through the pan and the
  pull-back, with stances right, and that includes locked linemen just after the snap. That is stills
  only; nobody has judged the motion yet.
- Before the snap the bodies are steady: joints move a median 1.6 cm a frame, no flips in 1,298 steps. In
  play 79 of 2,368 steps flip, a turn past 60 degrees in 1/25 s, and most come back within 5 frames. The
  rate follows size, not crowding: 0.2% on boxes over 110 px, 10.7% under 55 px. The camera pulls back
  in the middle of the play, and 31 of the 79 fall in 15 frames there.
- My first test for bad bodies was wrong. Body height over box height flagged 15% of bodies before the
  snap, and they looked fine: a crouched lineman's box often holds only his back, and the body rightly
  runs past it. Distance from the camera works, because a body drawn too big comes out too near. Short
  of 0.8 of its track's running distance: 69 bodies, 65 of them in play, 12 of 12 random ones wrong by
  eye. The boxes held part of a man or two men, and the model drew a giant, up to about twice the size.
  That rules these in, not out; it does not say how many it misses.
- My height number was also wrong, a y-extent in camera axes that a crouch and the camera's tilt both
  shorten. Bones cannot change with pose: thigh 0.38 m, shin 0.35 m, within 4% in a track. They are
  short. Against the real camera the bodies sit at 0.81 of the true distance, so they are about a fifth
  too small and too near, which the overlay hides since the two cancel on screen.
- What this settles for GRD-59: fitted bodies are good enough to carry on. Three things have to come
  first: filtering a track over time to take out flips, the giant check, and standing bodies on the field
  by their feet in yards at a real height, since the model's own distance is short and moves about 0.9 m a
  frame. Fits on disk hold vertices and joints only, so filtering in pose space needs the pose
  parameters saved too. A whole-game run waits for the shield retrain, which changes the boxes.
