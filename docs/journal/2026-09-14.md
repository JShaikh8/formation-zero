## Session 1 — Film on an external SSD

- The owner's laptop has no room for full games; the film lives on an external SSD. Made that a
  setting rather than a workaround: a film root (`FZ_FILM_ROOT`) holds the big files (source,
  proxies, clips) while the small ones (play tables, records, manifests) stay in the repo. A
  guard refuses to write when the drive is not mounted, so nothing lands on the boot disk by
  accident. `fz-film add` links a download into place without copying it.
- Both renditions of the demo game registered from the drive: 2.9 GB at 576p and 18.6 GB at
  1080p. Probed: 244,505 frames, 25 fps, 163 minutes, identical in both, which confirms the
  frame-aligned-proxy assumption on real footage. The source has a keyframe only every 6.7 s;
  the proxy will put one every second so seeking inside it is exact.
- Reordered the stages: the film window is found on the source first, and the proxy encodes
  only that window (about 96 of the 163 minutes) instead of the whole recording.
- The first real film-window scan is running against the summer's hand-found answer (film from
  media 2677 s to 8452 s). Result in the next entry.

## Session 2 — First real film, end to end, and where it stopped

- Film-window detection rewritten to use motion and brightness instead of colour, because the
  snow game's field reads as 0.1% turf. On the real 163-minute recording it finds the film at
  44.5 to 140.5 minutes, within half a minute of the hand-found answer at both ends, in 84 s.
- A clear game arrived: 49ers at Rams, week 1 of this season, played under the roof in
  Melbourne. Registered from the drive, probed (114,068 frames), film window found in 30 s
  (1.0 to 75.0 minutes), 540p proxy of the window built in 12.5 minutes, official play list
  pulled from the NFL API (141 filmable plays; no free labels exist yet for a game this new,
  which is the case the NFL-API-backbone decision was for).
- Shot detection ran on the proxy in 53 s and found 406 takes, the same over-segmentation
  shape the demo game had. Two things then failed to transfer from the demo game, and both are
  worth writing down:
  1. The cut threshold. This recording's cut scores run twice the demo game's, and camera pans
     raise them too, so an absolute threshold is wrong here. A cut is an isolated spike against
     its neighbours; measured that way the count lands near the 282 takes the play list implies.
     Not yet implemented.
  2. The camera-angle feature. The summer's edge-orientation number read 88 and 47 on the snow
     game and reads 2 to 6 and 15 to 33 here; on the snow game it was measuring graphics and
     upright players, not yard lines. Two hand-built replacements failed in the same afternoon:
     a yard-line orientation measure that turned out to measure the two cameras' colour balance,
     and a two-cluster split on global colour features that put half the sideline takes in the
     wrong cluster. Contact sheets of the real takes (one is in the gallery's future) show the
     difference is obvious to a person and to any learned model, and not to a hand-tuned rule.
- Conclusion: camera-angle labelling becomes the first milestone 2 task, done the milestone 2
  way: a contact sheet of takes, one keystroke each, a tiny classifier on downscaled frames,
  the alternation prior on top. That is the semi-automatic labelling loop on its first real job.
- State of the chain on the new game: film window, proxy and takes are done; play pairing and
  the join to the official list wait for the classifier.
