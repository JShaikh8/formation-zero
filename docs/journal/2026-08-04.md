The demo game arrived: Rams at Bears, 2025 week 20, as a 96-minute screen recording of NFL+
All-22. One long file, each play shown twice (sideline, then end zone), snow falling from about
the 65-minute mark. First job: turn that into 184 bounded plays.

## Session 1 — Shot segmentation

- Cut detection by the change in brightness between sampled frames, downscaled to 64 by 36
  pixels. The threshold had to be calibrated against the film's own score distribution: a value
  that "looks reasonable" found almost nothing; 15 separated cleanly.
- Recorded a cheap geometric feature per take, the dominant edge angle, as raw signal for later.
- Result: 419 takes over 96 minutes.

## Session 2 — Ground truth, and two failed classifiers

- Two hand-rolled sideline-versus-end-zone classifiers looked convincing on a sample and then
  landed near chance across the full film. The snow changed the field's contrast mid-game and
  broke the feature they relied on.
- Stopped guessing. Hand-labelled camera angles for a sample drawn from both halves of the
  film. Observation that changed the approach: adjacent takes alternate angle 37 out of 37 times.

## Session 3 — The play index

- Labelled every take with a sequence model (Viterbi) that lets confident neighbours decide an
  ambiguous take. Held-out accuracy 92 to 96 percent against 84 for a threshold.
- Merged same-angle neighbours (spurious cuts inside one take): 419 takes collapsed to 368,
  which is 184 plays times two angles. Paired them, joined them in order to the play-by-play.
- The count matched the official play list exactly. Nothing in the code arranged that; it fell
  out. First play begins at the first frame of film; the last play contains the overtime
  game-winning field goal at the media time it is known to occupy.
- Made the first four commits to the repository.

**Honest caveat.** An interior off-by-one would keep the count right and misattach every later
play. The index is strong evidence, not proof, until interior plays are checked against
something external.
