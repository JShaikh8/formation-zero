## Session 1 — The NFL API becomes the backbone

- Direction from the owner: the NFL's own API is available and already integrated in another
  of the owner's projects, so it becomes the single source of play data.
- Read that integration. The game-detail endpoint gives every play a stable id, down, distance,
  yard line, play type, description, and a list of every player who touched the play with their
  jersey number. Rosters give jersey and position for everyone. That is the whole identity
  problem's supervision, from one official source.
- Blueprint updated: NFL API adapter planned as a Python port; the July second-source client
  parked in place; nflverse kept for the formation and personnel labels the NFL API does not
  carry.

## Session 2 — SAM 3, and how to label without drawing boxes

- Question from the owner: isn't SAM 3 better? Answer: yes, as the teacher. It is the best open
  model for finding things by name, and its sibling SAM 3D Body gives 3D body pose from one
  image. But 135,000 frames per game at a few frames per second on a laptop is 19 hours of
  detection. So SAM 3 makes the labels; a small fast model trained on them runs every frame.
- Designed the semi-automatic labelling path: sampled frames, teacher proposals, one-keystroke
  review sheets, student retraining until it agrees with the teacher. Field keypoints need zero
  clicks: the known field grid is projected back into easy frames and becomes the label.
- Explained "hosted vision budget" plainly: each request to the hosted model costs money; about
  $2 per game if we only ask about hard plays, about $35 if we ask about everything. Starting
  cap: $5 per game.

## Session 3 — Formation Zero, and this tracker

- The project has a public name: Formation Zero. The Python package keeps its working name.
- Built the companion tracker: markdown and JSON in the repository are the source of truth, a
  small build script compiles them into a static site, and Render will redeploy it on every
  push. Pages: where we are now, the day-by-day journey, the plan (mirrored to Jira keys), the
  technology, the decision log, costs, a gallery, and a glossary.
- The Drive: progress drawn as a football field. Ten milestones, ten yards each. Blue line is
  where we are, yellow line is the next first down.

## Session 4 — One name

- Question from the owner: why is the package still called gridiron, and was that code good
  enough to keep? Answer: the code was worth keeping (small, tested, honest about its limits,
  and it records why every threshold is what it is). The second name was just a default.
- Renamed the package to formation_zero, the folder to formation-zero, and the commands to
  `fz-*`. Thirty files, fifteen minutes, 158 tests still passing. Decision D-019.

## Session 5 — What a play knows

- The owner read the plan and asked where routes, player direction, pre-snap movement, at-snap
  motion and formation, live routes and post-snap events were, and how a client would receive
  them. Fair: they were named as modules and never specified as an output.
- Wrote the play record: one versioned JSON document per play with pre-snap, at-snap, live and
  post-snap sections, an events timeline, and a per-player block with alignment, role,
  technique, stance, direction and body orientation. Every field carries its source and a
  confidence. Closed vocabularies for motions, routes, coverages, events and roles.
- Added a read-only JSON API over the same database the chat uses: one play, filtered plays
  across a game, per-frame positions, a frame snapshot, per-player history, and bulk export.
  Milestone 9, with the schema itself pulled forward into milestone 1 so clients can integrate
  before the vision work lands. Decision D-020.

## Session 6 — The film room, by hand

- Decided to build a demo of the end result now, not later, and only of the *output*: a
  hand-made play record and the views drawn from it, so the schema gets tested before it is
  expensive to change and every later milestone has a target.
- Built the film room. Home page with the two games we hold official play-by-play for; open
  a game and the real play list is on the left, grouped by drive, filterable by quarter, pass,
  run, or "broken down". Three real Rams–Bears plays are fully brought to life: the opening
  34-yard catch-and-run to DJ Moore with a tight end shift, Kyren Williams' goal-line touchdown
  run, and Stafford's deep shot to Higbee off jet motion. Their situation, personnel, formation
  label, passer, receiver, tackler and result are official; every path between those facts was
  authored by hand and says so.
- Four cameras over one renderer: bird's-eye, sideline, end zone, free camera. Trails, full
  routes, a scrubber with the three phases and every event marked, keyboard control, click a
  player for their card. The right panel reads the record out: before the snap, live, result,
  events; the players on the field; a scripted chat that answers from the record; and the JSON
  itself, with the per-frame table on request.
- Every other play shows the "official data only" state, which is exactly what a client sees
  before the vision work runs on it.

## Session 7 — Round two of the film room

- Owner's review of the demo: add separation for receivers, the same read from the defender's
  side, and how close active defenders got to the ball; and let people swap between our views
  and the real film to confirm the recreation. Agreed, plus a few of mine.
- Separation, coverage closeness, pursuit and quarterback pressure are now computed from the
  tracking table and summarised in every record (schema section 9.2a). Click a player and the
  card shows the number now, at the throw, at the catch, and the minimum, with a small curve
  through the play and a dashed line on the field to whoever they are measured against.
- Film is a fifth camera, frame-locked to the recreation, with a split mode that puts it beside
  any of our views. In the public demo it is a labelled placeholder; in the product it plays
  whatever clip the client is licensed to load.
- Chase camera (follow a selected player instead of the ball), an alignment card in the Play tab
  drawn from the record, confidence shown as a chip on every sourced field and as a dashed ring on
  players whose identity is pending, search over the play list ("Nacua", "89", "3rd & long",
  "red zone"), new filter chips (touchdowns, 15+ yards, shotgun, motion, play-action), and a
  "share moment" button that copies a link to the exact frame and camera.
